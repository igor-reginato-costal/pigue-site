Choices for a happy and comfortable home life

These are the kinds of details that make a home truly special: a door that you want to keep, music at the right volume for conversation, and a place to safely put down a hot dish. These are the choices that shape the character of each family's home.

Sustainability

A house that is worth keeping

Deciding what is worth salvaging and predicting how the house will be maintained after the move.

On the first visit, the family and architect choose what is worth restoring. Before each approval, Piguê explains how the choice affects cost, timing and everyday life.

Materials that could have another life

A solid wood door might only need new hardware. A stone countertop could be repurposed if measured before removal. When deciding what to keep and what to replace, the origin of the material, its durability, and the possibility of repair are important factors. The family can then choose a finish based on its current appearance and how it will age over time.

Please exercise caution during the construction work.

Maintaining the chosen flooring requires protecting it before the first team arrives. Starting with a stone slab involves planning the cuts with the stonemason. It is in these details that coordination of the work helps to avoid losses and rework. The same care guides the safety of those who are carrying out the work, the separation of leftover materials for reuse, and the recording of the destination of the waste.

Comfort after the move

The shade of a tree can protect the living room in the late afternoon. A well-placed window can bring in a breeze without sacrificing privacy. When choosing climate control, it's important to know which areas receive sunlight at different times and how the family uses them. Similarly, the distance between the water heater and the shower is important when planning for hot water: the water that runs while the shower heats up also counts towards the bill. In the garden, choosing plants that are well-suited to the location helps determine the amount of watering they actually need. The house manual provides instructions for use and repair for those who currently care for the property, as well as for future residents.

The carbon footprint of the house

A portion of greenhouse gas emissions occurs before the change, during the manufacturing and transportation of materials, and during the construction process. Another portion comes from the energy used for living and from the components that will need to be replaced. When comparing the renovation of a floor with the installation of a new one, for example, we consider the service each option provides and for how long. When data is available to estimate emissions, the comparison should clearly state which stages were evaluated and what assumptions support the result.

For Piguê, ensuring the well-being of future generations also means leaving them with a home that they can understand, maintain, and adapt.

Residential Technology

Precision behind comfort

Combine light, sound, and temperature to allow each person to use the house according to their own preferences and schedule.

As late afternoon light filters through the curtains, creating a warm glow for those seated at the table. Later, music accompanies the conversation on the veranda, while the rooms remain quiet. Piguê begins by understanding these moments and what each family wants to preserve within them.

Light, sound, and temperature

The lighting can be adjusted between dinner and reading. The living room and the balcony can play the same music, each at a volume appropriate for the conversation. In the master bedroom, even the air circulation is designed based on the bed's position. A button near the headboard can lower the curtains and turn off the main light, while leaving the reading lamp on for those who are still reading.

Connection, security, and privacy

If the family frequently works on the balcony, the Wi-Fi needs to reach the armchair even with the glass doors closed. At the entrance, the video intercom, access control, and pathway lighting are designed together. The camera's framing should allow identification of who is arriving while also preserving the privacy of those inside.

The care that goes into a home before work begins

In the BIM model, the team can check if there is enough space in the ceiling for the air duct, curtain track, and speaker box, including space to access the motor for maintenance. With these options clearly visible, the family decides which home automation features they want to use from the outset and which they prefer to leave for the future.

Appliances

A kitchen designed specifically for the cook.

Define countertops, appliances, and passageways based on how the family cooks and does laundry.

Before designing the cabinets, it's important to involve the person who uses the kitchen the most. Will the largest appliance fit in the oven? Where will it be placed when it's hot? The same considerations apply to the coffee maker you use every morning and the cast iron pot that has been passed down through generations. In a new construction or renovation, these items help the family, their architect, and the Piguê team to decide where to place countertops, outlets, and equipment.

Cooking while continuing the conversation

In an open-plan kitchen, it's important to consider the range hood's speed, which should be similar to what you would use when making a sauce. If there is an exterior outlet, the design can include an exhaust duct. When the air needs to return to the kitchen, the choice of filters and access for maintenance should be considered. If you are considering an induction cooktop, try out your favorite pots and pans to see if there is enough space to use two of the largest simultaneously.

From the refrigerator to the table, from the table to the sink.

The person who prepares meals for the week might want space in the freezer to stack portions. The person who hosts on Sundays might prefer a wide shelf to store an entire dessert. Imagine the refrigerator door open and someone walking through. After lunch, do the same exercise with the dishwasher open. Is it possible to carry the dishes to the sink and then put them away without having to go around the island?

For wine enthusiasts, a cellar with two temperature zones allows you to keep different groups of bottles at varying temperatures. The initial decision should be based on the types of wines you typically open and where you prefer to serve them.

How laundry moves through the home

Think about the task of changing your bed linens. A washer-dryer combines two tasks into one machine, but the load it dries may be smaller than the load it washes. With two machines, one can dry while the other is washing. Also, be sure to reserve space for the laundry basket and a surface for folding the sheets. These are details that continue to make a big difference long after the move.
