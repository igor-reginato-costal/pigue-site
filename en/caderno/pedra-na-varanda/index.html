Stone in the light it will live in

Concept study from the Piguê collection.

The small plaque, which appeared beige under the store's lights, takes on a grayish background under the eaves. This difference is significant: it is in that shadow that the stone will spend most of its life. Place the sample lying down, next to the wood and fabric that will accompany it.

When choosing a marble, ask to see the finished edge. The hand quickly understands what a profile drawing is trying to convey. Keeping the approved sample provides the family and the marble supplier with a common reference point when the entire piece arrives.

The "Between the Living Room and the Garden" showroom is where Piguê suggests comparing the stone to the wood from the veranda. The family can approve the sample they felt in the shade, having already known the price and delivery time for that particular choice.
