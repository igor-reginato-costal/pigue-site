Dressing rooms · Proposed retrospective edition · 14 Jun 2026

Black or navy?

Illustrative image

The jacket appears black on the hanger and navy blue next to the window. The difference becomes clear in the elevator, on the way to an appointment. Being able to identify a fabric in a closet is just as important as finding space to hang it.

In the closet lighting test, two items from the wardrobe itself reveal more than a single light fixture. With the jacket hanging on the hanger and the skirt on the bench, you choose the lighting that best highlights the two colors. The architect and carpenter can then adjust the position of the light to ensure that the open door or your shoulder does not cast a shadow inside the drawer.

The best experience with a tailor starts with how you choose your clothes. Someone who packs a suitcase on a bench needs to be able to maneuver around it. Someone who sorts their clothes the night before might prefer a single hook in a well-placed location. And someone who simply wants to keep a long dress for the pleasure of having it deserves a space where the hem can hang down completely.

The blue fabrics featured in the study "The Blue Room" appear on different types of surfaces. In the closet, Piguê would conduct the lighting test with the dark fabric hanging on a hanger and the door open, just as the resident would choose what to wear.
