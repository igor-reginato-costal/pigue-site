The guest who brings lunch

Illustrative image

The message arrives in the morning. "I made the lasagna you like. Can I leave it there around midday?" Receiving someone in the early days with a newborn can be a welcome surprise. The friend knows where the kitchen is, brought the covered dish, and has the whole afternoon to get to know the baby another time.

In a newly renovated apartment, it's helpful to consider the initial layout and how the spaces will function. For example, can the elderly relative easily access the kitchen from the entrance, without having to pass through the bedrooms? Can the living room be used for conversation while someone else relaxes in another area? A small table near the kitchen and a well-placed door between the living room and the hallway would give the family the flexibility to choose how they want to use the space on any given day.

Sometimes, the guest changes hands at the door. Other times, a friend comes over, cuts a few slices, and stays for a meal. What makes the house feel welcoming is the ability to decide between the two options when the doorbell rings.

The space behind the chairs at "The Table by the Veranda" allows you to move through the room with the table in use. When there is a baby at home, Piguê would also consider another route, the one used by the person bringing lunch and arriving at the dining area while the child is sleeping.
