One more chapter aloud

Illustrative image

The grandmother's voice changes when the fox appears. The little girl already knows what is coming and points to the illustration before the sentence is finished. For a library where this encounter takes place, the first piece of furniture to choose might be a seat where two people can see the same page.

There's an easy way to try it. While one person is reading, the other approaches to see the drawing near the fold. Does the cushion support both bodies? Does the light reach the paper or the head of the person leaning over obscure the fox? With the actual book in their lap, it's easier to choose the side of the lamp and the position of the outlet before closing the wall.

The lowest shelf can hold the books that the child already knows how to choose. When she gets down from the sofa to retrieve another one, the library becomes independent, no longer requiring an adult to decide how the story continues.

The reading in "The Courtyard House" begins with a child sitting on a sofa facing the tree. To read aloud, Piguê would practice the width of the seat with the illustrated book open between adult and child, with the lamp already turned on.
