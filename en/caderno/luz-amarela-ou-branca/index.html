The color of light · Proposed retrospective edition · 3 Nov 2026

Warm light or cool white?

Illustrative image

Do you know that feeling when dinner is over, but no one gets up from the table? There's still bread on the platter, someone pours another glass of wine, and the light makes the white tablecloth have a creamy hue. If you like that kind of atmosphere, look for a bulb with a color temperature between 2700 K and 3000 K. This is a good range to start experimenting with.

K stands for kelvin. It tells you about the color of the light. Around 2700 K, white light looks warm and yellow. Around 4000 K, it looks more neutral. At 6500 K, it takes on a bluer cast. This is color temperature. Two lamps can light a table equally well and give the same plates quite different appearances.

To choose, take the white plate you use every day. In the evening, place it under the pendant light and try different bulbs, using similar amounts of light. Then, sit in your usual spot and look at the person across from you. The decision usually becomes easy, as it's a matter of comparing the color of the plate with the color of the person's face.

In "The Table by the Veranda" the family chooses the view from the table. After dark, Piguê suggests trying out the pendant light with the family's dishes and seeing how the light falls on the face of whoever is sitting in front.
