Where parquet meets stone

Illustrative image

The wall now ends where there was once a door. On one side, the wood has gained a shine along the path between the window and the sofa. On the other side, the family wants a light-colored stone for the dining room. Anyone who knows the apartment still remembers closing that door on cold days. Even open, the passage might retain some remnants of the previous layout.

Before ordering the stone, the architect can mark on the floor where the two materials will meet. The team inspects the condition of the last pieces, their thicknesses, and the joint details. A small sample installed within the opening allows the family to observe the cut and feel, as they pass through, whether the opening is comfortable.

In the afternoon, the light first reaches the wood, and a few minutes later, it reaches the stone. This is a good time to choose the finish. In the same light, you can see what each surface brings to the room.

In "The Courtyard House" Piguê studies the transition between the stone and the door frame. During the encounter with an old mallet, this real-size trial allows him to feel the connection with his foot before approving the final finish.
