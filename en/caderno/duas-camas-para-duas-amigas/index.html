Two beds for two friends

Illustrative image

The two friends were attending the same wedding. One enjoys reading late into the night. The other goes for a run before breakfast and has already laid out her shoes. They share a room and have been doing so since college, and they know, without much discussion, who prefers the side with the window.

In the country house that is often used to host sisters, cousins, or friends, two single beds should be included in the first layout. With them positioned separately, each person can easily reach their glasses, turn on their light, and get out of bed without having to get up and around the other person's bed. If the family also plans to accommodate a couple, it's worth checking both layouts before installing outlets and lamps. A continuous headboard could accommodate both beds.

On Sunday, one bed is made before seven. On the other, a novel lies open, face down. Their host has known both women for years. She knows which side is whose.

Allowing guests to use the beds with existing occupants is an option offered by "The Table by the Veranda". Piguê would apply this approach to both beds, marking separate and adjacent positions on the floor before securing the reading areas.
