A film from the far end of the sofa

Illustrative image

There's a quick way to get to know a movie theater. Choose a seat at the very back, where there's usually plenty of space. From there, the light from the hallway might appear on the screen. The head of the last person to arrive might obscure the second row of subtitles. And the couch that seemed so comfortable in the center requires a bit of neck strain throughout the entire film.

Before installing the screen and ordering the seats, the architect can determine the optimal positions within the space. A dark scene with subtitles helps to determine the appropriate viewing angle. Another scene, with a low voice, is suitable for discussing what is being heard in the adjacent room. Each family has its own preferred viewing time. Often, it's Sunday evening, when most of the household is asleep.

If there's a favorite armchair, it should definitely be the first one to be drawn. The best place in the room is often the one where you already feel most comfortable.

In "The Curved Duplex" the double-height living room changes its lighting between day and night. If the family also watches movies there, Piguê would suggest watching a scene from the end of the sofa before setting up the screen.
