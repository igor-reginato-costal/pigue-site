A study beyond the garden

Illustrative image

Virginia Woolf wrote part of her work in a small pavilion in the garden of Monk's House, in Sussex. To reach the writing desk, one had to leave the house. It's a short distance, but anyone who has tried to resume a sentence after three interruptions knows how easily one's thoughts can be derailed in such a short space.

In a country house in the interior of São Paulo, a separate office can be useful for those who write, draw, or need to leave unfinished work until the next day. Before choosing the most beautiful spot on the property, it's worth imagining a rainy Tuesday. Where do the sheets of paper come from the house? Does the eaves protect those who wait at the door for the key? Can you see a visitor approaching from the seat you're in?

By the end of the afternoon, the notebook might still be open on the same page. The door is closed, you walk through the garden and arrive home for dinner. The next morning, the phrase remains there.

In "The Stable House" Piguê studies how to separate the living areas of the house from the support areas. For an office in the garden, the family could choose with the architect where visitors would be greeted and where the person working in the office would return for dinner.
