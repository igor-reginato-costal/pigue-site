Living rooms and shared spaces · Proposed retrospective edition · 22 Oct 2026

A sofa for the last one up

Illustrative image

Two friends finish their dinner and take the glasses to the living room. One takes off her sandals and crosses her legs on the sofa. The other prefers the armchair, where she can rest her feet on the floor. The conversation continues for a long time. It's unlikely that they would have chosen the same seat in a store.

When you try a sofa, take the book you’re reading. Turn a few pages, find a place for your elbow, reach for a glass. Then ask someone to sit beside you. Some sofas are wonderful for watching a film, yet make it surprisingly hard to talk without turning your neck.

In September interviews with US professionals, Houzz noted an interest in rooms assembled over time, with pieces from different eras. A starting point for your own living room may already be at home. Before ordering a suite, bring your favorite armchair over to the fabric sample. See where the two friends would choose to sit.

The two armchairs from "The Blue Room" encourage conversation because they face each other. By coordinating a room that also includes a long sofa, Piguê can test this arrangement with the family before making decisions about outlets and side tables.
