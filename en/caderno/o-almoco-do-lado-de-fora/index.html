Lunch outdoors

Illustrative image

In the country house, the best window in the living room overlooks an old tree. It's also the place where the family would like to have lunch when it rains. Before choosing the design of the veranda, it's worth sitting on the sofa, at the usual time, and seeing how much sky is visible through that window. That view is already part of the house.

In São Paulo, the Pinacoteca building, designed in the 19th century by the Ramos de Azevedo office, underwent a renovation by Paulo Mendes da Rocha in the late 1990s. It serves as an example of how a building from a different era can be adapted to new uses. On a smaller scale, the question is where the new roof can be installed without depriving the family of the natural light they enjoy.

A temporary marking of the roof's height helps to determine this by looking from the sofa. Afterwards, it's up to the team to decide on the support and the water drainage system. Once the first rain comes, those at the table can continue their lunch while still looking at the same tree.

The transition from the living room to the garden is central to the concept in "Between the Living Room and the Garden" When renovating an old house, Piguê would begin by assessing the path from the street to the table, in order to discuss with the architect which existing opening could accommodate this new use.
