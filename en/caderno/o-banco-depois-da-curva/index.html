The bench around the bend

Illustrative image

On the first pass through the garden, it seems natural to place the bench facing the most attractive flowerbed. On the second pass, the resident pauses a few steps further on. From there, in the shade, she listens to the rustling leaves and sees the veranda between two tree trunks. This is where she often stands, even though there is no place to sit.

Before establishing the final path, it's a good idea to bring a chair to this point and return at the time of day when the house is usually quiet. The morning sun might have moved on after lunch. Sitting down also allows you to see where the path needs a section that is firm and level to reach the bench without stepping on the flowerbed. The backrest and armrests make it easier to stay for another ten minutes.

Sometimes, you don't even need to bring a book. Sometimes, the best thing to do is simply listen for the first mango to fall from the tree and try to guess, based on the sound, whether it landed on the ground or on the roof.

The bench in the shade is one of the options proposed for the "Between the Living Room and the Garden" area. Piguê would suggest that the landscape architect visit the curve of the path during the break time, in order to determine the best position for the bench, taking into account who will actually be sitting there.
