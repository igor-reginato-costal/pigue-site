Why did that lipstick change color?

Illustrative image

In the bathroom, the lipstick appears to be a shade of purple. In the elevator, it pulls towards brown. The first instinct is to turn on a bright white light in front of the mirror. However, two lamps with the same white light can show the lipstick and the skin tone in different ways.

In addition to the Kelvin rating, also look for the IRC (Color Rendering Index) on the bulb's label. This indicates how closely the colors will appear under the light compared to what we would see under a reference light. For the mirror you use for getting ready, an IRC of 90 or higher is a good starting point. The color temperature can be as warm or cool as you prefer.

When trying out a lipstick, use one you're familiar with. Apply a small amount to your hand and observe it in front of a mirror. Then, move closer to the window. If two shades that you can distinguish in daylight appear the same in the bathroom, it's worth testing another light source before ordering the pair of sconces.

In "The Blue Room" the blue of the porcelain, curtains, and armchairs all need to coexist under the same light. For the mirror, Piguê would conduct this test by having the resident stand in front of it, with her usual makeup and lipstick on.
