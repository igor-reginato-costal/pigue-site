A table that begins with flour

Illustrative image

Making pasta by hand requires some coordination. While one person rolls out the dough, the other is already preparing to cut the first sheet into strips. Flour is spread across the table. One corner needs to remain dry to create the nests. It's easy to understand why, in some families, the large wooden table remains the first piece of furniture to enter the house.

For those who prepare dough this way, a continuous countertop section is worth more than it appears on the plans. The sheet must pass under the roller without encountering the sink in the middle. If the dough machine is the type that attaches to the edge, bringing it to the carpenter for consultation will resolve a precise detail. The countertop thickness should allow the clamping mechanism to close properly.

Around lunchtime, just one person needs to oversee the cooking process. The others can continue with their tasks, separating the pasta strands and discussing which ones are the thinnest. Half the enjoyment of serving the pasta has already been achieved at this point.

In Piguê's study, "The Table by the Veranda" there is enough space to move around with the table fully set. For a family that makes pasta, we would extend this space to include the countertop, with room to move the pasta strands from one end to the other without crossing the pot.
