Bath time · Proposed retrospective edition · 13 Nov 2026

A bath with a view into the treetops

Illustrative image

The first thing the resident wanted to keep was the view of a jacaranda tree. From the hallway, all you could see was the front building. The jacaranda tree occupied the entire window, and was located just to the left of the shower, one step away. In the spring, she knew that the tree would have already bloomed before she even left the house.

The bathroom window requires two observations, one from the inside and one from the outside. Marking the height of the user's eyes on the wall helps in choosing the right glass panel. Further down, a sample of textured glass allows for assessing how visible the view remains through it. At night, with the bathroom lights on, the team and the resident can re-evaluate the visibility lines before making a decision.

If the height is correct, the morning begins with a detail that no finished sample can reveal. Simply lift your face slightly to see if the tree has turned green or yellow overnight.

In Piguê’s Courtyard House study, the view of the tree was designed from the inside out. For a bathroom, we would try that view from the shower and then walk through the garden to check privacy from outside.
