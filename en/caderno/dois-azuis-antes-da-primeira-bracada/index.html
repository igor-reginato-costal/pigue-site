Swimming pools · Proposed retrospective edition · 6 Aug 2026

Two blues before the first stroke

Illustrative image

A sight of a blue tile in the palm of your hand might appear green when viewed from the bottom of a swimming pool. Between these two moments, the water, depth, sky, and the shadows of the trees come into view. Choosing the tile during a meeting leaves the most aesthetically pleasing part of the decision to the imagination.

In a country house in the interior of São Paulo, the color selection process begins with the landscape architect. They can bring in samples of the planned vegetation and the team can bring in samples of the cladding materials. Seeing the samples wet, from the veranda where the family often has lunch, helps to choose between two nearly identical shades of blue from the catalog.

On the edge, the decision is different. The meeting point between the stone and the floor needs to receive the water from the pool and provide a comfortable path to the shower. A section built to scale allows the architect to review this meeting point before ordering all the pieces. Then, you simply choose which end of the bench to place the towel.

In the study "The Stable House" the family is having lunch under the eaves, near the pool. Piguê would suggest comparing the two wet blues from this vantage point, taking into account the shade and vegetation that will be visible near the water.
