Where water meets your hands

Illustrative image

Make a small indentation with your hands, as if you were washing your face. It's in this small space between the faucet and the sink that you can determine if the faucet is the right size. A faucet that is too tall for a shallow sink can cause splashing. If it's too short, your fingers will end up touching the bottom or the back wall.

A countertop faucet occupies part of the countertop or the sink itself. A wall-mounted faucet leaves the countertop free around the sink and requires that the water outlet be positioned at the correct height and distance. Before drilling into the countertop or installing the sink, it is advisable to overlay the faucet and sink drawings from the side. This makes it easy to see where the water jet will fall.

The bathroom, which only has cold water, requires a simple faucet. The ensuite bathroom, with both hot and cold water, features a two-handle faucet that allows you to control the flow and temperature of each tap independently. A single-handle faucet combines flow and temperature control into one lever. Try operating it with one hand and moving the lever through the full range of motion, including backward, next to the mirror.

Piguê uses the interaction between the floor and the track in "The Courtyard House" to illustrate the importance of using two pieces together. In the sink area, the pair would consist of a faucet and a basin, with space for hands before drilling into the stone.
