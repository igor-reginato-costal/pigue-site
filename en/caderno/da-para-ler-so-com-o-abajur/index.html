Is the bedside lamp enough to read by?

Illustrative image

The house is quiet now, the book is good, and only a few pages remain to finish the chapter. You like the warm, yellowish light from the lamp, but you start to lean the book forward to see better. First, try moving the lamp closer and directing the light towards the page. Sometimes, this small movement is enough to allow you to rest your back against the armchair again.

If you still need more light, look at the two numbers on the bulb. You can maintain the 2700 K, which gives the room the desired warm tone, and choose more lumens, which measures the amount of light emitted. A dark shade allows little light to escape from the sides. An adjustable reading lamp helps to focus the light on the pages.

Perform the test with the book open in your lap, in the position you normally read in. Adjust the lamp's focus until both pages are clearly visible and the light doesn't obscure your vision. If someone has slept nearby, also note where the light beam ends. The last page may be well-lit while the rest of the room remains in dimness.

In "The Courtyard House" a lamp provides light when the patio's natural illumination decreases. In the artwork, Piguê can help determine the optimal position and angle of the lamp relative to the book being read, which is held in the lap of the person using the armchair.
