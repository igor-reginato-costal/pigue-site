Moqueca, served in its pot

Illustrative image

When the moqueca arrives, it also brings with it the pot. The broth still simmers along the edges, the cilantro perfumes the table, and the first spoonfuls go to those who were patient enough to wait. In Goiabeiras Velha, in Vitória, the pot makers hand-shape the clay pots associated with the cuisine of Espírito Santo. The Iphan (National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage) registered this craft in the "Book of Knowledge" in 2002.

This type of serving dish changes the conversation about the kitchen design. If the family uses it for Sunday lunches, it's worth considering when choosing the stove and checking with the supplier to ensure compatibility. At the table, a sturdy stand that protects the tabletop allows the server to easily access the dish. There is enough space for the serving spoon and the dish it holds, without interrupting the conversation.

When there's a little broth left over, someone always asks for another spoonful of rice. At that point, having left the pot in the center of the table was the best decision for the meal.

The table under the eaves of "The Stable House" brings the lunch closer to the barbecue. If the house's signature dish is moqueca, Piguê would help the family determine the best place to set down the hot pot so that everyone can serve themselves comfortably while seated.
