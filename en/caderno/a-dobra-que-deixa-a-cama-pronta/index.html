The fold that makes the bed

Illustrative image

At Grandma's house, anyone entering the bedroom knew exactly how to get into bed. She would fold the embroidered blanket over the duvet and leave one corner open. Her granddaughter adopted this method in her own apartment. In busy days, it only takes two minutes to do so.

First, she stretches the elastic sheet, fitting two opposite corners before adjusting the rest. Then, she centers the sheet on top, with the embroidered side facing the mattress. She allows the sides to hang evenly and secures the foot with some slack for the fingers. Finally, she places the duvet on top, slightly below the headboard. When she turns the sheet over, the embroidery is visible on the right side.

Finally, there are the sleeping pillows, already in their covers. The duvet can be folded up on the seat if the night is warm. From the doorway, the bed has a green line, a white bar, and a corner waiting for someone to lie down.

In "The Blue Room" a family heirloom guides the design of an entire wall. Piguê could start with an inherited embroidery to match the architect's design for the bed height, and leave the hem visible, allowing the person to fold it in the way they prefer.
