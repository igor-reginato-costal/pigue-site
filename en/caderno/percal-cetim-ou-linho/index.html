Percale, sateen or linen?

Illustrative image

Three sheets of bedding on the bed say more than three photographs. The cotton percale has a matte surface and a more defined crease. The cotton satin allows the light to follow the weave, and the fabric glides between the fingers. The two can have the same composition. What changes is the way the threads are interwoven.

"Linho" is the name of the fiber. In a freshly laundered linen sheet, the texture is usually visible, and the creases create soft folds. For those who enjoy making their bed by hand, these creases can be part of the appeal. However, those who prefer a crisp, well-defined edge may be drawn to percale. In addition to the composition on the label, it's also worth checking how each piece should be washed and whether the care instructions fit into your household routine.

If you're unsure, start by testing the pillowcase. Feeling the fabric against your face for a few nights can help you decide if that's the texture you'd prefer for the entire bed.

In "Between the Living Room and the Garden" Piguê suggests choosing the veranda stones based on touch. In the suite, a pre-washed pillow can guide another material selection, and when folded, it provides the carpentry workshop with the actual height of the game pile.
