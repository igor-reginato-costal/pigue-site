The glass cabinet and the sound of the room

Illustrative image

The crystal display case arrived at the apartment with the key in the lock and a small scratch on the left foot. The owner knows which move caused the scratch. The sound system that she uses daily will be placed next to it. The record chosen for the first night is already set aside.

The design can begin with the doors of the cabinet open. The width of the doors indicates how much space to leave until the new furniture. Lower, it matches the height of the person choosing the music. The carpentry accommodates the handles, provides access to the connections, and ensures the ventilation required for the equipment. This means that the next time the equipment needs to be replaced, it can be done entirely within the cabinet, which was designed specifically for it.

The final decision is made through sound. In the initial bars, a cup bumps against a saucer, creating a faint noise behind the glass. The owner moves the cup to a different shelf, closes the door with the old key, and listens again.

In "The Blue Room" the existing furniture and layout of the room guide the design. Piguê would treat the cabinet as part of the overall composition, experimenting with the porcelain within it before finalizing the placement of the boxes.
