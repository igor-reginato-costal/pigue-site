The doorbell in the studio

Illustrative image

From her headphones, engrossed in a drawing, she didn't hear the first doorbell. The second time, it was her daughter in the room next door who woke up. In that house, increasing the volume would solve the problem for the wrong person.

A doorbell can activate a light signal in the workshop. The most important decision is where it is located. Sitting at the workbench, the resident first notices the wall near the door. By the window, the signal is visible in the brighter light. Before deciding on the location, the team can test the signal in both morning and late afternoon sunlight. The sound in the room can remain active during the hours chosen by the family.

A friend rings the doorbell. She notices the light, finishes drawing the curtains, and goes to open the door. For those in the room, the visit only begins a few minutes later, when the voices of the two arrive from the kitchen.

In "The Curved Duplex" the lighting is assessed from the perspective of the person sitting there. For the workshop, Piguê would coordinate with the installer to conduct a test of the lighting at the occupant's eye level, both in the morning and at dusk.
